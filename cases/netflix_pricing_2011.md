# Netflix Pricing And Subscriber Flows, 2011

- Case ID: `CASE.NETFLIX.PRICING.2011`
- Domain: BUSINESS / Subscription Economics
- Primary concept: Subscription Economics Foundation
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

How could Netflix add 4.714 million U.S. subscribers in Q3 2011, end the quarter with 0.805 million fewer unique subscribers, and still grow U.S. revenue 44% year over year?

## Minimum Unit

One paying household-month under a specific plan and realized price. A quarter contains multiple customer-months from households that can start, migrate, continue, or cancel at different points.

## Narrative Structure

- Before: A popular $9.99 plan combined streaming and one-DVD-at-a-time service.
- Pressure Builds: Streaming and DVD had diverging cost structures and strategic futures, leading Netflix to separate plans.
- Turning Point: In July 2011 Netflix priced streaming-only and one-DVD-only at $7.99 each, making both $15.98, about 60% above the former combined plan.
- Cascade: Q3 began with 24.594 million unique U.S. subscribers, added 4.714 million, recorded 5.519 million cancellations, and ended at 23.789 million.
- Decision: Management retained separate pricing while reversing the proposed Qwikster rebrand and acknowledging that transition speed and communication damaged relationships.
- Outcome: U.S. Q3 revenue reached $799 million, up 44% year over year, because period revenue reflected active customer-months, pricing and plan mix while many cancellations arrived late in the quarter.
- Aftermath: Netflix continued its streaming transition, but the episode remains a clean demonstration that acquisition, cancellation, ending stock, price and period revenue are distinct quantities.

## Subscription Mechanism

Ending stock equals beginning stock plus additions minus cancellations under the company's period definitions. Period revenue is generated across all active customer-months and realized prices, not by multiplying ending stock by the latest list price. Cohorts are needed to distinguish plan, tenure, acquisition channel, and price-shock effects.

## Numbers That Change The Interpretation

- $15.98 versus $9.99 is a $5.99 absolute increase and roughly a 60% relative increase for households keeping both services; it did not apply identically to streaming-only or DVD-only users.
- 4.714 million gross additions did not produce growth because 5.519 million cancellations were larger.
- 23.789 million ending unique subscribers were a stock measured at quarter end, not the quarter's customer-month denominator.
- $799 million of U.S. revenue and 44% year-over-year growth did not prove long-run relationship health because cancellations were back-loaded and future customer-months had already been lost.

## Evidence Boundary

The public filing gives aggregate subscriber flows and management's interpretation, not household-level causal effects of price, communication, and Qwikster separately. The case demonstrates accounting relationships and timing; it does not prove every price increase causes churn or that subscriber decline always reduces current-quarter revenue.
