# Three Mile Island Unit 1: High Capacity Factor, Retirement, and Proposed Restart, 1974-2026

- Case ID: `CASE.TMI1.CAPACITY_FACTOR_ECONOMICS.1974_2026`
- Domain: SYSTEM / Energy Systems
- Primary concept: Capacity Factor and Utilization
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

How could a nuclear unit operating near a 94% capacity factor retire for economic reasons, then become the subject of a restart plan supported by a 20-year corporate power purchase agreement?

## Before

TMI-1 was a pressurized-water reactor that began commercial operation in 1974. It was distinct from Unit 2, which suffered the 1979 partial meltdown. Nuclear plants carry high fixed staffing, maintenance, security, regulatory, and site costs, so long stable runs spread those costs across more megawatt-hours.

## Pressure Builds

During the 2010s, prolonged low wholesale prices, competition from low-cost natural gas and renewables, slow demand growth, and TMI's relatively high single-unit-site costs weakened revenue. EIA historical data record one year with 805 MW net summer capacity, 6,633,750 MWh generation, and about a 94% capacity factor, showing that poor utilization was not the central problem.

## Turning Point

TMI failed to clear three consecutive PJM Base Residual Auctions. In May 2017, Exelon announced a 2019 retirement absent policy changes, citing five years of financial losses, low wholesale prices, capacity-auction outcomes, missing compensation for zero-emission attributes, and site-specific costs.

## Cascade

The company stopped long-term capital projects, canceled future fuel and outage planning, and prepared employee and decommissioning actions. PJM lost an 803 MW operating resource, while replacement effects on price, reliability, and emissions depended on the marginal mix of generation, transmission, demand response, and future investment.

## Decision

The operating question was not whether a high capacity factor was desirable. It was whether energy, capacity, policy, and other revenue could cover fuel, variable O&M, fixed O&M, required capital, and financing. Under the then-current structure, Exelon judged continued operation uneconomic.

## Outcome

TMI-1 permanently ceased power operations on September 20, 2019. Fuel was certified removed from the reactor vessel on September 26, 2019. High utilization and retirement therefore coexisted: capacity factor described operating output, not project profitability.

## Aftermath

In September 2024, Constellation announced a 20-year PPA with Microsoft to support restarting the unit as the Crane Clean Energy Center. The company estimated about $1.6 billion in restart capital and a target around 2028. NRC review, equipment restoration, upgrades, and inspections remain necessary; a contract does not itself authorize restart.

## Quantitative Reality

- Historical EIA table: 805 MW net summer capacity.
- Historical annual generation: 6,633,750 MWh.
- Implied capacity factor: about 94.1%.
- Each 1 percentage point at 803 MW: about 70,343 MWh per ordinary year.
- Permanent shutdown: September 20, 2019.
- Proposed renewed capacity: approximately 835 MW.
- Microsoft PPA: 20 years.
- Company-estimated restart capital: approximately $1.6 billion.

## Causal Judgment and Evidence Boundary

The evidence supports high operational utilization alongside unfavorable economics. Exelon's loss and policy claims came from an interested owner and do not independently establish the causal contribution of each market rule. The later PPA supports a change in revenue certainty, but the restart has not yet proven future capacity factor, cost, reliability, schedule, or regulatory approval.

## Transfer

For any capital-intensive asset, first calculate potential output and actual output. Then separately explain the utilization gap and test whether the revenue generated by actual use covers the full fixed, variable, capital, and contractual cost boundary.
