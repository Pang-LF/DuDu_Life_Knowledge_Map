# Charter-Disney Distribution Negotiation, 2023-2025

- Case ID: `CASE.CHARTER.DISNEY.DISTRIBUTION.2023_2025`
- Domain: BUSINESS / Subscription Video, Pay-TV, and Streaming
- Primary concept: Media Industry Power Shift
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

If Disney could distribute directly to consumers and Charter could retain households through broadband, why could neither party bypass the other without material cost?

## Minimum Unit

One Spectrum household seeking ESPN during the 2023 blackout. Its choice to wait, switch distributors, buy another streaming service, cancel video, or retain only broadband converts content scarcity and customer access into bargaining consequences.

## Narrative Structure

- Before: Disney supplied scarce sports and a portfolio of linear networks; Charter supplied aggregated household reach, packaging, billing, service, and broadband access.
- Pressure Builds: Cord cutting reduced linear scale while programming rates and separate DTC products increased pressure for package flexibility and included streaming value.
- Turning Point: The contract expired and Disney programming left Spectrum, forcing both companies' outside options into actual customer, fee, advertising, and service consequences.
- Cascade: Charter avoided $61 million of programming cost during the temporary loss but later reported that video disconnects were partly driven by the dispute; Disney lost linear reach and affiliate and advertising value.
- Decision: The parties restored 19 networks, removed eight, included wholesale DTC entitlements, enabled packaging flexibility, and used Charter's broadband base for DTC retail distribution.
- Outcome: Core ESPN distribution remained while the agreement reallocated weaker networks, streaming access, packaging, and customer acquisition roles.
- Aftermath: A 2025 expansion added Hulu and returned the eight networks under a larger bundle, showing that bargaining outcomes change when the full exchange changes.

## Power Mechanism

Bargaining power comes from relative no-deal costs, asset substitutability, switching speed, and time sensitivity. Disney's scarce live sports raise Charter's churn exposure; Charter's large video and broadband relationships raise Disney's reach and revenue exposure. DTC and broadband strengthen outside options without making either side costlessly independent.

## Numbers And Boundaries

- Charter told investors it expected to pay Disney more than $2.2 billion in 2023 for carriage rights; this was Charter's expected payment, not Disney profit.
- The $61 million benefit from temporary programming loss was avoided programming cost, not net economic gain after customer and operating consequences.
- Charter lost 248,000 residential video customers in Q4 2023 versus 145,000 a year earlier and said the difference was partly driven by the Disney loss; not all 248,000 disconnects can be attributed to Disney.
- The 2025 statement's more than $100 monthly retail value sums service list prices and is not Charter's per-household wholesale payment or every customer's willingness to pay.
