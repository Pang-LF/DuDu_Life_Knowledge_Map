# France: Nuclear Electricity and Energy Accounting Boundaries, 1973-2024

- Case ID: `CASE.FRANCE.NUCLEAR_ENERGY_BOUNDARIES.1973_2024`
- Domain: SYSTEM / Energy Systems
- Primary concept: Energy Accounting Boundary
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

How could France generate 95% of its electricity from low-carbon sources in 2024 while fossil fuels still supplied about 57% of final energy?

## Before

Before the 1973 oil shock, imported petroleum supported transport, heating, industry, and part of electricity supply. These uses appeared in different end-use sectors but shared exposure to one internationally traded primary-energy source.

## Pressure Builds

The 1973 oil shock rapidly increased oil prices and made import dependence a strategic constraint. A country could not eliminate every petroleum-using device quickly, but it could change the conversion source feeding the national electricity network.

## Turning Point

On March 6, 1974, Prime Minister Pierre Messmer announced an accelerated nuclear programme. France subsequently built a standardized pressurized-water-reactor fleet, converting fission heat through steam turbines into electricity.

## Cascade

Nuclear generation displaced oil and other fossil generation in the electricity sector, but gasoline vehicles, aviation, gas heating, and industrial combustion did not automatically electrify. The electricity mix and economy-wide final-energy mix therefore diverged.

## Decision

After electricity became predominantly low-carbon, further economy-wide decarbonization increasingly required a different intervention: replace fossil end-use equipment, expand networks and low-carbon supply, improve efficiency, and address services that are difficult to electrify.

## Outcome

- Total French electricity generation in 2024: 539.0 TWh.
- Nuclear generation: 361.7 TWh, or 67.1% of generation.
- Low-carbon generation: 95% of generation.
- Fossil electricity generation: 20.0 TWh.
- Economy-wide final energy: 1,498.3 TWh.
- Final petroleum products: 586.8 TWh; natural gas: 262.0 TWh; coal: 6.6 TWh.
- Fossil final energy: about 855.4 TWh, or 57.1%.
- Final electricity: 399.8 TWh, or 26.7% of final energy.

## Aftermath and Accounting Convention

France's 2024 physical energy balance counted 1,152.9 TWh of nuclear primary energy but RTE recorded 361.7 TWh of nuclear electricity. Nuclear primary energy is counted as reactor heat, with roughly two-thirds lost in thermal conversion. Wind, hydro, and solar primary energy are counted at electricity output under the direct-equivalence method. Primary-energy shares therefore depend partly on accounting convention.

## Causal Judgment and Evidence Boundary

The nuclear programme clearly transformed the electricity supply structure and reduced fossil generation. Annual aggregate shares do not identify every effect on prices, welfare, health, security, or emissions, and they do not describe hourly system conditions. The remaining fossil final-energy share supports an electrification interpretation but does not by itself rank every replacement technology.

## Transfer

Any energy claim should be rewritten as numerator over denominator, then labelled by conversion stage, geography, time window, and convention. Electricity-generation success should neither be generalized to the entire economy nor dismissed because other sectors remain fossil-dependent.
