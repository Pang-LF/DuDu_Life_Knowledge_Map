# Good Judgment Project Calibration, 2011-2015

- Case ID: **CASE.GOOD_JUDGMENT.PROJECT.CALIBRATION.2011_2015**
- Domain: META / Forecasting
- Primary concept: Calibration and Resolution
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

If a skilled group gives a low probability to an event and it occurs, did the event disprove their forecasting skill?

## Minimum Unit

One question opened on 2011-09-01 and asked whether a lethal confrontation involving government forces would occur in the South or East China Sea by 2011-12-31. Top forecasters began near 20% and reduced the probability as time passed. A South Korean coast guard officer was killed on 2011-12-11, so the event resolved true.

## Scale And Design

- The first two tournament years covered 199 resolved geopolitical outcomes.
- Year 1 began with 2,246 participants; Year 2 with 1,648.
- Questions stayed open for 102 days on average, ranging from 2 to 418 days.
- The top 2% from Year 1, 60 people, entered five teams of 12 in Year 2.
- Probability training lasted about 45 minutes; training and team conditions included randomized assignment.

## Mechanism

Forecast records make probabilities and outcomes comparable. Brier scoring penalizes confident errors. Calibration compares probability bins with observed frequencies; resolution tests whether higher-probability events happen more often than lower-probability events. Repeated feedback, probability training, team critique, updating and performance-based aggregation turn judgment into a learning system.

## Quantitative Anchors

- On the surprise maritime question, trained teams averaged a 1.44 two-outcome Brier score versus 1.31 for untrained independent forecasters.
- Across the larger set, probability training, teaming and top-performer tracking improved performance.
- Forecasts of 100% made early in a question were correct about 70% of the time; 100% forecasts near the end were correct about 90%, showing overconfidence at the extreme.
- IARPA later released a four-year dataset containing millions of forecasts.

## Evidence Boundary

The case does not show that a low-probability event's occurrence is irrelevant; the individual record should receive a poor score. It shows that probability quality is a property of a set of frozen forecasts, not one outcome. Participants were selected and highly educated, geopolitical questions differ from corporate and personal domains, and top-performer persistence mixes ability, engagement, selection and team environment.
