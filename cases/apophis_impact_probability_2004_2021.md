# Apophis Impact Probability, 2004-2021

- Case ID: `CASE.APOPHIS.IMPACT_PROBABILITY.2004_2021`
- Domain: META / Probability; exposure to Planetary Defense
- Primary concept: Probability Foundation
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

Why did Apophis's estimated 2029 impact probability move from about 2.7% to ruled out when the asteroid itself did not change trajectory in response to the forecast?

## Minimum Unit

One time-stamped optical or radar observation constrains, but does not perfectly determine, the asteroid's position and velocity. Many weighted orbit solutions can remain compatible with a short and noisy observation arc.

## Narrative Structure

- Before: Apophis was discovered in June 2004 with only a short optical observation arc.
- Pressure Builds: As observations removed many safe orbital solutions while retaining some Earth-intersecting solutions, the April 13, 2029 impact probability briefly rose to about 2.7%.
- Turning Point: Pre-discovery archival images lengthened the observation baseline and excluded a 2029 impact.
- Cascade: Arecibo radar reduced the 2029 predicted-position uncertainty by about 98%, while subsequent optical and radar observations constrained later keyhole encounters.
- Decision: Maintain monitoring, seek observations with high information value, separate near approach from impact, and revise public risk estimates as the information set changes.
- Outcome: 2021 Goldstone and Green Bank radar observations enabled CNEOS to rule out an Earth impact for at least 100 years and remove Apophis from the Sentry risk table.
- Aftermath: Apophis will still pass about 32,000 kilometers above Earth's surface in 2029, inside geosynchronous altitude, creating a rare scientific opportunity without an impact threat.

## Quantitative Anchors

- Peak reported 2029 impact probability: about 2.7%, roughly 27 per 1,000 or 1 in 37 under the then-current information and model.
- Complement under the same model: 97.3% no impact on that date.
- Radar effect: about 98% reduction in predicted 2029 position uncertainty after high-precision Arecibo measurements.
- Size: mean diameter about 340 meters; long axis at least about 450 meters.
- 2029 close approach: about 32,000 kilometers above Earth's surface.
- 2021 radar distance: about 17 million kilometers from Earth; radar image pixels represented about 38.75 meters.

## Evidence Boundary

The “orbit cloud” is an intuition for a weighted statistical uncertainty distribution, not a claim that the asteroid physically occupies many trajectories. Risk estimates depend on the observation set, measurement-error model and dynamics. Ruling out impact for 100 years is a model- and horizon-bounded statement, not metaphysical certainty forever.
