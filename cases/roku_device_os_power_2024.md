# Roku Device and Operating-System Power, 2024

- Case ID: `CASE.ROKU.DEVICE_OS_POWER.2024`
- Domain: BUSINESS / Subscription Video and Streaming
- Primary concept: Device and Operating-System Power
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Question

How can Roku gain industry power without owning most third-party programming or the household broadband connection?

## Minimum Unit

One viewer turns on a Roku-powered TV, arrives at the home screen, discovers a title, opens or installs an app, signs up or returns, and watches. Different layers create home-screen advertising, subscription or transaction revenue share, billing control, first-party data and video advertising opportunities.

## Mechanism

Roku places its OS in households through players, Roku-made TVs and licensed TV OEM designs. The home screen appears before app choice and after app exit; cross-app recommendations, search and sports destinations reduce discovery cost. Content partners can buy promotion, use platform billing and share subscription, transaction or ad economics. Low device margins can therefore acquire a longer platform relationship.

## Quantitative Anchors

- 89.8 million Streaming Households at year-end 2024, up 9.8 million.
- 127.1 billion Streaming Hours in 2024, up from 106.0 billion, about 19.9%.
- $3.523 billion platform revenue versus $590 million devices revenue, roughly six times as large.
- $1.886 billion platform gross profit versus an $80.3 million devices gross loss.

## Evidence Boundary

Streaming Households are active accounts, not paid subscriptions or people. Segment totals do not identify device-cohort LTV, the causal contribution of a specific home-screen feature, confidential partner revenue shares, ranking motives or consumer welfare. Roku's bargaining power varies with partner brand strength, alternative devices, app availability, privacy constraints and the user's ability to switch.
