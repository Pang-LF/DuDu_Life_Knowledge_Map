# Tesla Model 3 Production Ramp, 2017-2018

- ID: `CASE.TESLA.MODEL3_RAMP.2017_2018`
- Domain: META / Systems Thinking
- Date studied: 2026-09-12
- Primary concept: Bottleneck
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

Why did Model 3 output remain at only 260 vehicles in Q3 2017 when several production subsystems had demonstrated much higher burst rates?

## Before

Tesla aimed to move from lower-volume premium vehicles into a mass-market production system. Management targeted 5,000 Model 3 vehicles per week and pursued extensive automation.

## Pressure Builds

Tesla produced 25,336 total vehicles in Q3 2017, but only 260 were Model 3. Drive units, seats, paint, and stamping had demonstrated burst rates above 1,000 vehicle sets per week, while complete synchronized throughput remained far lower.

## Turning Point

Tesla identified battery module assembly at Gigafactory 1 as the primary constraint. Four modules were required for one Model 3 battery pack, and the first two of four module-production zones required major redesign.

## Cascade And Decision

Without a complete battery pack, additional bodies and other components became work in process rather than finished cars. Tesla redirected engineering talent, temporarily reduced automation in selected areas, used semi-automated or manual processes, scheduled downtime for changes, and later added general-assembly capacity.

## Outcome And Moving Constraint

- Tesla reported more than 2,000 Model 3 vehicles per week for three consecutive weeks near the end of Q1 2018.
- Q2 2018 Model 3 production was 28,578, nearly three times Q1; the final seven days reached 5,031.
- Q3 production was 53,239, with an average of about 4,300 per week excluding planned shutdowns.
- As production stabilized, outbound delivery logistics became a major challenge, showing that the constraint had moved.

## Bottleneck Mechanism

Every completed vehicle required one complete pack, and each pack required four modules. Excess effective capacity in stamping, paint, seats, or drive units could not substitute for missing module capacity. Improving a non-bottleneck increased work in process or idle time; improving the current constraint raised feasible system throughput until another step became limiting.

## Comparison And Evidence Boundary

Burst rate is not sustained synchronized throughput, and a single final-week peak is not the same as a stable weekly average. Production counts and management diagnoses come from Tesla SEC filings. Public data support the constraint mechanism, but do not isolate the exact output contribution of any one manual process, automation change, or engineering intervention.
