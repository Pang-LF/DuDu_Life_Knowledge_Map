# Hornsdale Power Reserve: Flexibility, Ramping and Storage, 2017-2020

- Case ID: `CASE.HORNSDALE.FLEXIBILITY_STORAGE.2017_2020`
- Domain: SYSTEM / Electricity / Flexibility
- Primary concept: Flexibility, Ramping and Storage
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

Why could a 100 MW / 129 MWh battery, capable of roughly 75 minutes at full output, create meaningful grid and market value without being able to sustain a multi-hour regional shortage by itself?

## Before

South Australia needed local frequency-control capability when loss of the Heywood Interconnector was credible. AEMO recorded that the resulting 35 MW local regulation-FCAS constraint had historically produced prices above AUD 9,000/MWh because few local suppliers could respond.

## Pressure Builds

Hornsdale entered service in late 2017 with 100 MW discharge, 80 MW charge, and 129 MWh storage, sharing a 275 kV connection with a 300 MW wind farm. Commercial and reliability arrangements allocated 30 MW and 119 MWh to normal energy operation, 70 MW of discharge capacity to reliability, and 10 MWh to a System Integrity Protection Scheme.

## Turning Point

On 18 December 2017, 689 MW of generation tripped in New South Wales. AEMO reported that Hornsdale's observed response was fully consistent with the expected contingency response and that tests and simulations confirmed response faster than conventional synchronous generation.

## Cascade

Hornsdale also followed AGC set-points that could be refreshed up to every four seconds and provided rapid, accurate regulation service. In Q1 2018 it charged 11 GWh and discharged 8.9 GWh, was enabled for regulation FCAS 71% of the time and contingency FCAS 99% of the time, and participated in all eight then-existing NEM FCAS markets.

## Decision

The operator had to allocate charge, discharge, headroom, footroom, state of charge, reserved energy, and recovery across energy arbitrage, regulation, contingency response, and SIPS. Overlapping enablement did not create duplicate physical MW or MWh; every commitment remained jointly constrained by the same inverter and battery state.

## Outcome

During a five-hour South Australian local FCAS constraint on 14 January 2018, AEMO estimated that added Hornsdale supply reduced regulation-service cost by about AUD 3.5 million, with average raise and lower regulation prices around AUD 248/MWh rather than the historical above-AUD 9,000/MWh pattern. Across Q1 2018, total NEM FCAS cost was AUD 25 million, AUD 32.7 million or 57% below Q4 2017.

## Aftermath

Hornsdale expanded by 50 MW / 64.5 MWh in 2020 and later demonstrated grid-forming and enhanced frequency-response capabilities. Market design also evolved toward products that distinguish faster response, illustrating that technical performance creates revenue only when products measure and reward it.

## Causal Judgment and Evidence Boundary

AEMO identified Hornsdale and EnerNOC demand response as key contributors to lower FCAS prices, but also recorded fewer hours of the South Australian constraint, lower offers from incumbent suppliers, and additional low-priced supply. The 57% quarterly cost reduction is therefore not a clean estimate of Hornsdale's independent causal effect. Likewise, charged and discharged quarterly GWh do not identify round-trip efficiency without beginning and ending state, service activation, auxiliary load, and a consistent meter boundary.

## Transfer

Flexibility is a vector, not a nameplate number. Evaluate direction, baseline, power, response speed, duration, location, state, recovery, and failure correlation; then verify that the service stack is jointly feasible before counting revenue.
