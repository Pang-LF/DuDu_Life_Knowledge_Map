# U.S. Traditional Pay-TV Cord Cutting

- ID: `CASE.PAYTV.CORD_CUTTING`
- Domain: BUSINESS / Subscription Video, Pay-TV and Streaming
- Date studied: 2026-09-12
- Primary concept: Cord Cutting System Effect
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

Why does one household's cancellation affect programmers, rights holders, remaining subscribers, and product strategy instead of only reducing one distributor's customer count?

## Scale

The FCC reported 101.6 million traditional MVPD subscriptions at the 2012 peak and 54.1 million at the end of 2023, a decline of about 47.5 million or 47%. The count fell by 7.8 million, about 12.6%, during 2023 alone. Traditional MVPD household penetration declined from about 88% in 2009 to about 41% in December 2023. These figures exclude vMVPD subscriptions and do not mean that former subscribers stopped watching video.

## Money And Contract Mechanism

A household cancellation removes recurring video revenue from the distributor. Some per-subscriber programming and service costs also disappear, so the full bill was never pure profit. Programmers lose affiliate-fee and linear-reach bases, while multi-year sports rights and production commitments may adjust more slowly. Responses such as higher per-subscriber rates, thinner packages, DTC products, and cost reductions then change the value and price faced by remaining households.

## Company Evidence

- Disney reported domestic ESPN affiliate and subscription fees of $10.837 billion in fiscal 2025. A positive 7% contractual-rate effect was almost offset by a negative 7% subscriber effect.
- Domestic ESPN programming and production costs rose 8% to $11.240 billion, primarily reflecting higher rights and production costs; those costs support all ESPN distribution and cannot be assigned only to linear subscribers.
- Comcast reported 11.270 million domestic video customers at year-end 2025, down 1.253 million in one year, while domestic broadband customers were 31.255 million.
- Charter reported $8.8 billion of programming costs in 2025, down from $9.7 billion in 2024 due mainly to fewer video customers and package mix, partly offset by contractual rate increases.

## Comparison

Switching from one traditional MVPD to another preserves a payer inside the legacy multichannel system while reallocating distributor share. Leaving traditional MVPD for a small set of DTC services removes that payer from the old affiliate-fee and distribution structure, although video consumption continues elsewhere. Moving to a vMVPD weakens traditional cable or satellite distribution but retains an internet-delivered multichannel intermediary.

## Evidence Boundary

FCC, Disney, Comcast, and Charter figures use different definitions and periods and should not be added mechanically. Company explanations support the mechanism but do not reveal confidential affiliate rates or isolate the exact causal effect of each household. Cord cutting is one driver among pricing, content availability, service design, competition, and household preferences.
