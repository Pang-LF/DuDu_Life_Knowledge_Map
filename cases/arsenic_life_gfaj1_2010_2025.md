# GFAJ-1 Arsenic-Life Claim, 2010-2025

- Case ID: `CASE.GFAJ1.ARSENIC_LIFE.2010_2025`
- Domain: META / Source Evaluation
- Primary concept: Claim–Evidence Fit
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Question

Why did institutional prestige and peer review fail to establish that arsenic replaced phosphorus in bacterial DNA?

## Evidence Chain

The original study grew GFAJ-1 under high arsenate and low-phosphate conditions and interpreted growth and sample signals as evidence that arsenic entered phosphorus-bearing biomolecules. NASA communication amplified the strongest interpretation. Independent teams separated arsenate tolerance, growth dependence and covalent DNA incorporation into different testable claims.

## Quantitative Anchors

- Follow-up conditions included 40 mM arsenate and 3 μM added phosphate, a nominal concentration ratio of about 13,333 to one; medium concentration did not identify molecular location.
- Reaves et al. used ten replicate 300 μL cultures per growth condition.
- Purified-DNA analysis found trace free arsenate but no detectable covalently bound arsenate.

## Outcome

Independent 2012 studies described GFAJ-1 as arsenate resistant but phosphate dependent. Science retracted the original paper in 2025. The organism remained scientifically interesting, while the core DNA-substitution claim did not.

## Evidence Boundary

Retraction does not by itself prove fraud or invalidate every observation. The decisive update came from more direct measurements, alternative-explanation control and independent replication. NASA's role and Science publication are metadata about provenance and process, not substitutes for claim-specific evidence.
