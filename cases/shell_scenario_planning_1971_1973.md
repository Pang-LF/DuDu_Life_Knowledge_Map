# Shell Scenario Planning and the 1973 Oil Shock

- Case ID: **CASE.SHELL.SCENARIO_PLANNING.1971_1973**
- Domain: META / Forecasting
- Primary concept: Scenario Planning
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Question

How could Shell prepare for an oil shock without predicting the exact war, embargo date or price?

## Scenario Structure

Shell's early 1970s scenario work separated rising producer-country power, import dependence and slow supply adjustment from uncertain triggers such as government coordination, conflict and embargo. A September 1972 presentation reportedly used six river-like branches and explored shifts in producer attitudes, OPEC power and a possible Middle East shock.

## Quantitative Anchors

- Saudi light posted price before the embargo: about $3.01 per barrel.
- October 16, 1973 posted price: $5.12.
- Announced price from January 1, 1974: $11.65.
- $11.65 divided by $3.01 is about 3.87, a near-quadrupling in under three months.
- Shell's retrospective dates the initial scenario work to 1971-1973 and the key management presentation to September 1972.

## Decision Use

The scenarios did not supply a precise point forecast. They challenged the assumption of effectively unlimited supply, let managers rehearse a producer-controlled world, and supported discussion of robust buffers and contingent asset decisions.

## Outcome

War, embargo, production cuts and price increases made one family of mechanisms highly relevant. Shell says the work improved readiness and supported valuable refinery and installation decisions, but public evidence does not isolate scenario planning's causal contribution from other management and asset differences.

## Evidence Boundary

This is not proof that scenario planning predicts rare events. A useful scenario must be specified before resolution, contain causal distinctions and produce signals and decisions; writing many vague stories and selecting the closest one afterward has little evidential value.
