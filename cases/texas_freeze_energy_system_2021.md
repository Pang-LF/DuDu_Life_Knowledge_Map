# February 2021 Texas Freeze as an Energy-System Failure

- Case ID: `CASE.TEXAS.FREEZE.2021`
- Domain: SYSTEM / Energy Systems
- Primary concept: Energy System Foundation
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

How could the largest U.S. crude-oil and natural-gas-producing state lose electricity and heating service for millions of residents?

## Before

ERCOT relied on a diverse generator fleet, including 64,202 MW of installed natural-gas capacity, or 52.2% of its installed capacity at the time. The natural-gas and electric systems were efficient but interdependent: gas supplied generators while electricity powered parts of gas production, processing, and transport.

## Pressure Builds

Extreme cold increased electricity and heating demand while freezing wellheads, gathering and processing facilities, generator equipment, sensors, valves, and wind turbines. From February 8 to 17, Lower-48 natural-gas production fell 28%. At the February 17 low point, Texas, Oklahoma, and Louisiana produced about 21 Bcf/d less than the January 2021 average, a decline exceeding 50%.

## Turning Point

Unavailable generation and rising demand threatened real-time grid balance. ERCOT ordered about 20,000 MW of controlled load shedding to prevent a wider grid collapse.

## Cascade

Across the event area, 1,045 distinct generating units experienced 4,124 outages, derates, or failures to start. Freezing caused 44.2% of events and fuel issues 31.4%; natural-gas supply accounted for 87% of fuel issues. Failures at gas production and processing reduced generator fuel, while electric outages could also affect gas infrastructure.

## Decision

Operators had to allocate limited supply, protect grid frequency, identify critical gas and public-service loads, and decide which customers to disconnect. Restoring electricity to a gas facility did not guarantee immediate gas output when equipment was frozen or connected to shared distribution circuits.

## Outcome

More than 4.5 million Texas residents lost power, some for as long as four days. Texas DSHS confirmed 246 storm-related deaths across 77 counties, including 158 hypothermia deaths.

## Aftermath

FERC, NERC, and regional entities recommended stronger winterization, weather data, capability declarations, gas-electric coordination, critical-infrastructure identification, and load-shedding plans. FERC approved additional extreme-cold reliability standards in 2023 and directed further improvements.

## Causal Judgment and Evidence Boundary

The evidence supports a multi-path energy-system failure, not a single-technology explanation. Official event reconstruction identifies timing and failure categories but does not randomize winterization or market designs, so it cannot estimate every proposed reform's independent causal effect. The 61,800 MW event-area generation-loss figure is not a simultaneous peak outage and should not be converted into energy-not-served without a time profile.

## Transfer

Resource reserves, annual production, installed capacity, available power, delivered final energy, and useful service are different layers. Reliability analysis should follow one required service through the entire conversion chain and locate the constraint under the specified conditions.
