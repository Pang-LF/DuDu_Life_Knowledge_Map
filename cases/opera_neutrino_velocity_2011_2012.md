# OPERA Neutrino-Velocity Anomaly, 2011-2012

- Case ID: `CASE.OPERA.NEUTRINO_VELOCITY.2011_2012`
- Domain: META / Source Evaluation
- Primary concept: Epistemic Audit
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Question

How could 15,223 events and roughly six-sigma significance produce an apparent faster-than-light result that disappeared after timing-system correction?

## Measurement Chain

OPERA inferred neutrino travel time by combining CERN proton-beam timing, UTC and GPS synchronization, a long optical-fibre link to the underground master clock, local oscillator behavior, detector time stamps, a geodetic baseline and statistical alignment of time distributions. A shared timing offset could therefore move all events together.

## Quantitative Anchors

- Initial anomaly: about 60.7 ns early over roughly 730 km, equivalent to about 18.2 light-metres.
- Original analysis: 15,223 events from 2009-2011.
- Baseline precision: about 20 cm, equivalent to about 0.67 ns of light travel.
- Ordinary proton extraction: 10.5 μs, about 173 times the anomalous shift.
- ICARUS independent check: seven short-beam neutrino events consistent with light speed.
- Corrected OPERA result: 6.5 ± 7.4 ns statistical, +8.3/-8.0 ns systematic, consistent with zero.

## Outcome

OPERA identified oscillator and fibre-connection timing effects. Borexino, ICARUS, LVD and corrected OPERA measurements in 2012 were consistent with light speed. The collaboration published and corrected the anomaly while preserving its separate neutrino-oscillation program.

## Evidence Boundary

The case does not show that statistical significance is useless or that extraordinary results are impossible. It shows that significance is conditional on the measurement model and enumerated errors, and that independent paths can be more diagnostic than more observations through one shared system.
