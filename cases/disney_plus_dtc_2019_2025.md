# Disney+ DTC Customer Relationship, 2019-2025

- ID: `CASE.DISNEY_PLUS.DTC_2019_2025`
- Domain: BUSINESS / Subscription Video, Pay-TV and Streaming
- Date studied: 2026-09-13
- Primary concept: DTC Customer Relationship
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

When Disney moved programming into Disney+, did it truly remove the distributor and own the customer, data, and bill?

## Before

Disney historically reached households through theaters, retailers, content licensees, and traditional MVPDs. Those intermediaries handled much of acquisition, packaging, billing, technology, and service while Disney earned affiliate, advertising, and licensing revenue.

## Pressure Builds

Cord cutting reduced linear distribution, while streaming platforms accumulated direct accounts and viewing behavior. Disney acquired streaming capabilities through BAMTech, launched ESPN+ in 2018, assumed operational control of Hulu in 2019, and prepared Disney+.

## Turning Point

Disney+ launched on November 12, 2019 at $6.99 per month in the United States, with almost 500 films and 7,500 television episodes. Users could subscribe directly at DisneyPlus.com or through Apple, Amazon, Google, Roku, and other platforms; Verizon offered an entitlement to eligible customers.

## Relationship Mechanism

For a direct web subscriber, Disney controls more of price, account, payment, product usage, service, renewal, and cancellation. For an app-store or wholesale subscriber, acquisition, billing, identity, data, and revenue are divided by contract. The DTC product is direct in brand and experience, but not every paid subscription is directly billed by Disney.

## Cost And Decision

Disney gained first-party product interaction and pricing control while taking on technology, distribution, marketing, payments, customer service, content, and churn. It launched at a low price and invested heavily in scale, then used pricing, advertising tiers, bundles, product integration, and cost control to pursue profitability.

## Quantitative Outcome

- Disney+ reached 73.7 million paid subscribers by the end of fiscal 2020, less than 11 months after launch.
- The former DTC segment lost $4.015 billion in fiscal 2022, reflecting substantial programming, technology, distribution, and marketing costs.
- Entertainment DTC reported $24.614 billion of revenue and $1.327 billion of operating income in fiscal 2025, an operating margin of about 5.4%.
- The 2022 and 2025 segment definitions are not fully comparable because ESPN DTC reporting moved into the Sports segment.

## Comparison And Evidence Boundary

A direct DisneyPlus.com subscription, an App Store in-app purchase, and a Verizon entitlement all count as access to Disney+, but they do not allocate billing, acquisition, data, service, or revenue identically. Public filings include wholesale arrangements in paid-subscriber definitions and do not disclose each partner's economics or data rights.
