# Disney+ Pricing and Tiering, 2022-2023

- Case ID: `CASE.DISNEY_PLUS.TIERING.2022_2023`
- Domain: BUSINESS / Subscription Economics
- Primary concept: Pricing and Packaging
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Question

Why did Disney+ preserve a $7.99 entry price with advertising while moving the ad-free tier from $7.99 to $10.99 instead of applying one uniform increase?

## Product Change

- Effective December 8, 2022, Disney+ Basic with Ads launched at $7.99 per month.
- Disney+ Premium without ads moved from $7.99 to $10.99 per month, a 37.5% increase; annual Premium moved from $79.99 to $109.99.
- Basic retained the full content catalog, up to seven profiles, four concurrent streams and supported high-quality video formats, while downloads and several premium features were excluded at launch.
- Disney+ and Hulu with ads were packaged at $9.99; Disney+, Hulu and ESPN+ with ads at $12.99.
- More than 100 advertisers participated at launch.

## Reported Outcomes

- The U.S. Disney+ ad tier had 5.2 million subscribers as of September 30, 2023.
- Fiscal-2023 domestic Disney+ average monthly revenue per paid subscriber increased from $6.34 to $6.97, about 10%.
- Disney attributed the ARPU increase to retail pricing and advertising revenue, partly offset by a higher mix of multi-product offerings.
- Domestic Disney+ period-end subscribers were 46.5 million versus 46.4 million one year earlier.

## Mechanism

The ad tier preserved a lower price for price-sensitive users while adding advertiser payments. The premium tier charged more to users with higher aversion to advertising. Bundles offered discounts in exchange for a broader relationship. Plan-level value depends on subscription and ad revenue, service and ad costs, migration, retention and cannibalization rather than list price alone.

## Evidence Boundary

Public disclosures do not provide plan-level retention, advertising contribution, migration or cohort LTV. ARPU mixes retail, wholesale, promotional and bundle allocations. Content, marketing, economic conditions and other pricing actions changed simultaneously, so aggregate outcomes do not identify a causal effect of tiering alone.
