# California Resource Adequacy and Rotating Outages, August 2020

- Case ID: `CASE.CALIFORNIA.RESOURCE_ADEQUACY.2020`
- Domain: SYSTEM / Electricity / Resource Adequacy
- Primary concept: Resource Adequacy and Capacity
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

Why did California experience rotating outages when the August 2020 resource adequacy table showed the full 51,413 MW obligation had been met?

## Before

California's framework required load-serving entities to procure capacity against a 1-in-2 weather peak forecast plus a 15% planning reserve margin. For August 2020, the adjusted forecast was 44,740 MW and the total obligation was 51,413 MW, met through 48,926 MW of shown RA, 290 MW of RMR resources, and 2,197 MW of regulatory credits.

## Pressure Builds

A West-wide heat wave raised load and reduced the diversity of imports. The resource mix had also shifted the most difficult period from gross peak toward evening net-demand peak, when solar declined while air-conditioning demand remained high.

## Turning Point

On August 14, actual load plus demand-response add-back was about 4.8% above forecast, forced outages were about 4.8%, and contingency reserve need was about 6.3%. Operational need therefore exceeded the 15% margin; including 514 MW of unreplaced planned outages raised the gap to about 17% of forecast load.

## Cascade

At the 18:51 net-demand peak, wind and solar output had fallen 5,438 MW from the gross peak period. Day-ahead load was also under-scheduled. The overlap of demand tail risk, outages, import scarcity, resource timing, and scheduling reduced deliverable supply and reserve.

## Decision

CAISO could continue serving all load with deficient contingency reserves, risking uncontrolled separation after another loss, or shed load in a controlled way to rebuild reserves. At 18:38 it declared Stage 3 and ordered two 500 MW blocks of controlled load shed.

## Outcome

On August 14, about 1,000 MW was ordered shed for roughly one hour; about 491,600 CPUC-jurisdictional customers were affected. On August 15, about 500 MW was ordered shed for 20 minutes; about 321,000 such customers were affected. Utility restoration procedures extended some customer outages beyond the system-level order.

## Aftermath

The joint CAISO, CPUC, and CEC analysis identified three major contributors: extreme weather, resource adequacy and planning processes, and market practices. California added resources and revised procurement and market rules toward more granular time-of-day, local, and flexible adequacy requirements.

## Causal Judgment and Evidence Boundary

The evidence shows that a peak-focused paper capacity total did not fully represent deliverability in the evening risk period. It does not prove that solar, one generator, imports, under-scheduling, or any single rule independently caused the outages. Avoided-outage claims require portfolio simulation across weather, outage, import, duration, network, and behavior assumptions.

## Transfer

Redundancy must be evaluated in the failure state that matters. Server count, hospital beds, bank assets, and electricity nameplates all overstate resilience when correlated failures, missing complements, duration, or location prevent service at the stress moment.
