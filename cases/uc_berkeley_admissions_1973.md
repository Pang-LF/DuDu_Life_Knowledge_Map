# UC Berkeley Graduate Admissions, 1973

- Case ID: `CASE.UC_BERKELEY.ADMISSIONS.1973`
- Domain: META / Probability; exposure to Education and Stratified Analysis
- Primary concept: Conditional Probability
- Status: Studied; Teach-back pending

## Puzzle

How could women have a substantially lower aggregate admission rate while most large departments did not show a lower female admission rate?

## Minimum Unit

One application enters one department, receives one admit or deny decision, and belongs simultaneously to a gender group, a department-conditioned applicant pool and the campus aggregate.

## Narrative Structure

- Before: UC Berkeley received about 15,000 graduate applications for fall 1973; 12,763 complete applications across 101 departments or programs received decisions.
- Pressure Builds: 3,738 of 8,442 male applicants were admitted, 44.3%, versus 1,494 of 4,321 female applicants, 34.6%, a 9.7-point aggregate gap.
- Turning Point: Admissions decisions were decentralized by department, whose base admission rates differed sharply, so researchers conditioned the comparison on the decision-making unit.
- Cascade: In the six largest departments, 51.5% of men but only 7.2% of women applied to high-admission departments A or B; 92.8% of women applied to departments C-F.
- Decision: Campus aggregate rates answer an overall outcome question, while department-conditioned rates answer whether committees treated applicants differently within the same decision unit.
- Outcome: The original study found few units with statistically significant departures, about as many favoring women as men, and a small pooled bias favoring women after accounting for departments.
- Aftermath: The authors shifted concern toward earlier educational and social sorting, while the observational data could not by itself identify why applicants chose departments or prove the full system was fair.

## Quantitative Anchors

- Campus: men `3,738 / 8,442 = 44.3%`; women `1,494 / 4,321 = 34.6%`.
- Reverse condition: women among admitted applicants `1,494 / 5,232 = 28.6%`, which is not the female admission rate.
- Six largest departments: men `1,198 / 2,691 = 44.5%`; women `557 / 1,835 = 30.4%`.
- Department A: men 62.1%, women 82.4%.
- Department F: men 5.9%, women 7.0%.
- Application mix: 51.5% of men and 7.2% of women in the six-department table applied to A or B.

## Evidence Boundary

The table contains sex, department and decision, not individual qualifications or reasons for department choice. Department conditioning is relevant to committee decisions but does not measure inequality in prior education, occupational opportunity, socialization or application access. The data are from one 1973 cycle and cannot describe current Berkeley admissions.
