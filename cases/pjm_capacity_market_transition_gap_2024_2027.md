# PJM Capacity Market Repricing and Transition Gap, 2024-2027

## Case ID

`CASE.PJM.CAPACITY_MARKET.TRANSITION_GAP.2024_2027`

## Anchor Concept

`BUS.ENERGY.CAPACITY_MARKET_MISSING_MONEY`

## Before

The 2024/25 Base Residual Auction cleared 147,478.9 MW UCAP at $28.92/MW-day. PJM reported a 20.4% total reserve margin and approximately $2.2 billion in load cost. Thick historical surplus and low load growth had kept the marginal capacity price low.

## Pressure Builds

Load forecasts accelerated, especially from data centers; prior retirements reduced surplus; Winter Storm Elliott changed accreditation and performance assumptions; and new resources faced interconnection, local permitting, equipment, construction, financing, and policy risk. The planning need, accredited supply, and cost of entry moved together.

## Turning Point

The 2025/26 RTO capacity price rose to $269.92/MW-day, approximately 9.3 times the prior price. Cleared UCAP was 135,684 MW, total reserve margin 18.5%, and reported load cost $14.7 billion. The result exposed a steep portion of the capacity supply-demand intersection but was not a controlled comparison because forecasts, accreditation, zones, and rules changed.

## Cascade

The 2026/27 auction cleared at the FERC-approved $329.17/MW-day cap. RPM procured 134,311 MW UCAP and FRR entities committed 11,933 MW, only 139 MW above the projected requirement. PJM reported 2,669 MW UCAP of new capacity and uprates, about 1,100 MW of retirement withdrawals after the prior auction, and more than 5,400 MW year-over-year peak-forecast growth.

## Decision

For 2027/28, PJM used a temporary $333.44/MW-day UCAP price collar. 809.6 MW UCAP offered above the cap and did not clear. The collar limited customer and market-power exposure but also prevented some higher audited or opportunity-cost offers from clearing.

## Outcome

RPM cleared 134,478.1 MW UCAP and FRR committed 11,299 MW, totaling 145,777.1 MW. PJM reported this was 6,517 MW below the reliability requirement. Cleared IRM was 14.4% against a 20% target.

## Aftermath

PJM identified a transition gap involving load growth, past retirement, local permitting, equipment and construction lead times, cost inflation, accreditation, administrative parameters, and regulatory uncertainty. Gas turbines and generator step-up transformers had lead times approaching three to four years, while the reference-resource development schedule increased from about 24 to 44 months. PJM began considering backstop procurement and a broader review of energy, reserve, and capacity investment incentives.

## Evidence Boundary

- Auction prices, cleared MW, reserve margins, and stated load costs are reported facts from PJM documents.
- The shortfall report is PJM's operator interpretation, not an independent causal experiment.
- The 2024/25 through 2027/28 results are not a clean time series because load forecasts, resource accreditation, zones, offer rules, administrative caps, and participating portfolios changed.
- The evidence supports multiple converging causes. It does not show that removing the price collar alone would have delivered 6,517 MW on time.
- Announced new capacity and withdrawn retirements occurred after higher prices, but timing alone does not identify the price as their sole cause.
